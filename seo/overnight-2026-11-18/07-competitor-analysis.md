# Competitor analysis

**Method.** Eleven commercial queries were run on 18 November 2026 through a web-search tool (US-based, so it approximates rather than replicates google.co.uk and gives no rank positions). The domains that recurred were then fetched and analysed page by page. No backlink or authority figures could be obtained from login-free sources, so nobody below is called strong or weak on links. Claims competitors make about themselves (awards, client counts, results) are reported as claims. Full sources are in the [source register](source-register.md).

## The competitors that matter

### Softomate Solutions — the closest tactical threat
London "AI automation agency". The page title leads with a price ("Workflows From £1,500"). One 5,000-word page covers pricing, timelines (4–8 weeks simple, 8–16 complex), a comparison table, sector use cases and platform choices (n8n, Make, GoHighLevel). It publishes from-prices (care plans from £99/month; AI integration from £4,000) and two anonymised case studies with hours saved, and shows a named founder, company number and ICO registration. Its weak points are sprawl (ERP, testing and products sit alongside automation), many `-london` template pages, and "best agencies" listicles that rank itself first.

*What to learn:* price signals and named, verifiable company details convert. Template city pages and self-ranking lists are the part not to copy.

### Whitehat SEO — the pillar-and-cluster benchmark
A London marketing agency building an "AI consulting" identity: a pillar (`/blog/ai-consulting-uk`) with supporting cost, readiness-assessment, automation and case-study pages. It is unusually transparent on price (readiness assessment £5,000–£12,000; implementation £25,000–£60,000), which frames the market high. Its proof is weak ("200+ SMEs guided", no names) and some figures are inconsistent.

*What to learn:* a cluster of honest guides around one service page is what wins "AI consultancy for small business". Chinvat's prices would sit well below Whitehat's, which is an advantage if stated.

### Cloudswitched — breadth without proof
An IT managed-services firm with "custom AI software development" pages, cost guides (chatbot £3,000–£5,000; larger £15,000–£50,000+) and many programmatic service URLs, including SEO. No case studies, clients or reviews; its stat counters render as zero.

*What to learn:* topical breadth alone gets pages into results. Chinvat can match the topics with better writing and real specifics.

### Digital Adaption — the solo, operations-first consultant
A Wirral-based independent consultant: "Stop doing manually what software should already be doing." It focuses on manufacturing and ops-led SMEs on Microsoft Power Platform, uses a five-step method and an FAQ that includes "We tried automation before… can you fix it?", and shows a certification (PL-200) and 15 years' experience. No pricing and no testimonials, but it ranks for the business-process-automation consultant query.

*What to learn:* a one-person consultancy can rank for the core automation query with a clear, specific page. This is the strongest evidence that the new workflow page can compete.

### Slink — the short page with the sharp hook
"Replace the spreadsheet everyone is working around." A 500-word page lists five problem/solution pairs and six tool types, with no FAQ or pricing, and still surfaces for "internal tools development UK".

*What to learn:* a precise problem statement does more than length. The software page and the spreadsheet article lead with the same pain, in our own words.

### Pulsion, Ficode, Doublespark — established studios
- **Pulsion** (Glasgow, since 1995): automation, DevOps and staff augmentation, with near-duplicate blocks, no FAQ and no pricing. It wins on age and URL breadth.
- **Ficode:** "UK's Most Trusted Software Development Company", offshore-style delivery, prices from £10,000 to £200,000, ISO and Cyber Essentials badges, company number shown.
- **Doublespark** (Peterborough): a 5,000–7,000-word local-modifier page for web application development, real screenshots, anonymised case studies without numbers, and 4.9 stars from 15 Google reviews (mostly for web and SEO work).

*What to learn:* depth, screenshots and visible company details are the norm. None of them speaks plainly to a 10-person business.

### n8n Lab — tool specialism plus listicles
"London's Premier n8n Automation Agency", with city landing pages worldwide, case cards with metrics but no baselines, about 15 client logos, and self-ranking "best agencies" lists. Its footer buttons pre-fill AI-assistant prompts asking to cite n8nlab.io.

*What to learn:* tool specialism is winnable, since the n8n SERPs are thin and listicle-led, but only for a firm that genuinely delivers on that tool.

### SEO: Chris Lever, SEO Sherpa, ProfileTree
- **Chris Lever** (Manchester freelancer): £70/hour published, speaking and free tools as authority, no case studies.
- **SEO Sherpa:** enterprise. Publishes pricing (ongoing £500–£8,000/month, audits £1,000–£5,000), awards and team bios.
- **ProfileTree** (Belfast): wins "SEO for small business UK" through volume of SME guides; states "from around £300 to £1,200 a month".

*What to learn:* technical SEO consultancy is won on demonstrated expertise. Chinvat's £299–£799 retainers sit at the bottom of published ranges, which is attractive to SMEs if the deliverables are explained, and alarming if they aren't.

## Synthesis

### Table stakes Chinvat must match
1. A dedicated page per service with a clear H1 naming the service. **Done locally for all four.**
2. An FAQ that answers cost, time and risk. **Done**, without inventing numbers.
3. A process section. **Done** (the shared four steps, applied per service).
4. Some price signal. **SEO has it; the others don't.** Owner decision: publish "from" prices or typical ranges for a first project.
5. A UK GDPR and data-handling statement. **Done** in the AI and automation FAQs.
6. Named people and verifiable company details. **Missing**, blocked on the Companies House question.
7. At least one case study with specifics. **Missing.** Scornik Gerstein needs writing up, with permission.
8. A third-party profile (Google Business Profile, Clutch). **Missing.**

### Gaps Chinvat can exploit
1. **"Replace spreadsheets" has no credible UK answer.** Flagship article drafted.
2. **Candour beats unsourced claims.** Competitors' numbers rarely have baselines. Chinvat's pages say what isn't worth doing; keep that voice everywhere.
3. **Small first projects are under-explained.** Few competitors explain both a small first step and what drives the cost. The cost articles address this.
4. **Ownership and handover** are almost never stated. Owner decision on terms, then say it.
5. **One builder across website, automation, AI and SEO.** Competitors split these. The service pages cross-link to make the joined-up offer visible.
6. **Technical specificity.** Most competitor pages are generic benefit copy. Articles that show real working knowledge (webhooks, error handling, data mapping, crawl and rendering) stand out with buyers and with search engines.

### Where Chinvat will struggle, honestly
- Head terms ("AI integration services UK", "custom software development UK", "SEO agency UK") are held by directories and sites with hundreds of URLs. Expect long-tail wins first.
- Four service lines can read as "does everything". The answer is the one promise in [positioning](01-business-positioning.md) (connect, automate, build the missing piece), repeated consistently.
- Technical SEO credibility is won through speaking, tools and case studies that don't exist yet.
- Directories reward reviews, and there are none yet.

### What not to copy
Self-ranking "best agencies" listicles, city template pages, unverifiable metrics, and prompt-injection-style "summarise with AI" buttons. They are visible now, but they spend trust the business doesn't yet have.
