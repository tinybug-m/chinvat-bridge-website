## Brand & Style

Chinvat Bridge embodies the synthesis of monumental antiquity and frontier machine intelligence. Named after the legendary crossing of discernment and reckoning, the institution serves elite statecraft, defense, and sovereign infrastructure clients navigating epochal technological transitions. The visual language merges the cyclopean masonry of Persepolis, the axial discipline of imperial Roman fora, and the exactitude of modern systems engineering.

The aesthetic rejects ephemeral digital tropes: there are no floating gradient pills, neon accents, or casual SaaS cards. Instead, the interface conveys architectural weight, enduring order, and lapidary precision. Whitespace functions as structural courtyard space; borders resemble finely chased architectural incisions; typography bears the authoritative permanence of stone inscriptions paired with mathematical clarity.

Key stylistic imperatives:
- **Tectonic Symmetry:** Symmetrical framing, post-and-lintel structural bands, and explicit columnar logic guide information architecture.
- **Lapidary Inscriptions:** Roman numerals, tabular coordinates, and engraved monospaced glyphs signify structural indexing rather than whimsical decoration.
- **Architectural Permanence:** High visual mass, hairline bronze fillets, and planar field transitions replace drop shadows and blurred depth.

## Colors

The color system derives entirely from architectural minerals: travertine limestone, volcanic basalt, patinated imperial bronze, and burnt terracotta clay. The default state is a warm, unhurried light mode evocative of carved stone bathed in direct Mediterranean and Persian sun, with basalt dark states reserved for computational consoles and inner sanctum views.

### Architectural Canvas & Stone Tones
- **Travertine Primary (`#FBF9F5`):** The foundational field color. Used for panoramic body surfaces and uninterrupted page fields.
- **Limestone Bed (`#F4EFE6`):** The primary neutral container surface. Used for structural bays, column plinths, and elevated content slabs.
- **Honed Mortar (`#E8DFD1`):** Subdued boundary tone for dividers, baseline grids, and subtle framing strokes.

### Basalt & Basal Carbon
- **Volcanic Basalt (`#121110`):** The deepest anchor tone. Used for sovereign headers, primary typography, and primary button fills.
- **Chiseled Basalt (`#1C1B19`):** The core primary hue. Applied to elevated dark panels, navigation monoliths, and dominant borders.
- **Weathered Ash (`#54514B`):** Structural secondary typography, metadata labels, and inactive architectural lines.

### Metals & Imperial Accents
- **Imperial Bronze (`#9E7D47`):** The primary accent. Applied to rule lines, Roman indices, active structural indicators, and consecrated actions.
- **Antique Gold (`#B89758`):** Hover states, luminous indicators, and high-priority glyphs.
- **Imperial Terracotta (`#7A3828`):** Tertiary indicator for critical thresholds, sovereign warnings, and immutable cryptographic operations. Never used as a playful status pill; always as a restrained stone stamp.

## Typography

The typographical pairing relies on deliberate contrast between classical antiquity and computational rigor. 

- **Monumental Serif (`EB Garamond`):** Used for titles, section headings, and epigraphic inscriptions. Display sizes must remain dignified, set with restrained tracking and natural proportional spacing. Inscriptions and section markers use all-caps with generous tracking (`0.18em`), emulating Roman lapidary stone carving and Persepolitan friezes.
- **Architectural Sans (`Inter`):** Deployed for dense analytical readings, data layouts, form elements, and executive summaries. Its optical neutrality and mechanical clarity anchor the classical headlines in modern computational realism.
- **Roman Numerals & Inscription Badges:** Primary sections, phase gates, and technical milestones must be indexed with capital Roman numerals (`I`, `IV`, `IX`, `MMXXV`) rather than informal decimals, rendered in `label-inscription` bronze.

## Layout & Spacing

The layout model honors classical symmetry and axial alignment. Content is anchored within a disciplined 12-column architectural grid with structural colonnades and explicit horizontal architraves.

### Grid Architecture
- **Desktop (1440px and above):** 12-column system, max container width 1360px. Gutters set strictly to `2rem` (`colonnade-gutter`), page margins to `4rem` (`colonnade-margin`). Content bays are separated by `1px` continuous bronze or mortar hairlines.
- **Tablet (768px - 1439px):** 6-column system, gutters `1.5rem`, margins `2rem`. Structural symmetry collapses into alternating dual-column blocks.
- **Mobile (below 768px):** 4-column system, gutters `1rem`, margins `1.25rem`. Elements stack vertically along a single monumental axis; horizontal hairline dividers maintain rhythmic separation.

### Framing & Boundary Logic
- Layouts must avoid free-floating elements. Every content cluster must be bounded by a lintel (top hairline border) or seated upon a stylobate (bottom foundation rule).
- Vertical alignment lines should be maintained across component tiers; card-like items align edges directly against structural column lines rather than using loose margins.

## Elevation & Depth

This system intentionally eliminates blurred drop shadows, neomorphic bevels, and translucent backdrop filters. Depth is established exclusively through tectonic masonry layering, structural line incisions, and value contrast.

### Tectonic Planes
- **Ground Floor (Base Plinth):** Field travertine (`#FBF9F5`).
- **Elevated Stylobate (Layer 1):** Slabs of limestone (`#F4EFE6`) bordered by hairline (`1px`) lines of honed mortar (`#E8DFD1`).
- **Inner Sanctum (Layer 2 / Focal Modules):** Recessed panels of volcanic basalt (`#1C1B19`) with antique bronze (`#9E7D47`) hairline borders.
- **Overlays & Dialogues:** Full-opacity slabs with a continuous `1px` border of basalt or bronze. To indicate depth over underlying surfaces, dialogues use an opaque travertine fill coupled with an external `3px` solid offset frame line set `4px` outside the component boundary, evoking stone revetment detailing.

## Shapes

The shape philosophy is absolute and unyielding: **Zero Radius (`0px`)**. 

Curvature is antithetical to cut stone and classical entablature. Every button, input field, modal, card boundary, and indicator badge is cut with razor-sharp 90-degree corners. 

Chamfered cuts (45-degree corner notches of `4px` to `8px`) are permitted exclusively on primary architectural badges, seal containers, and institutional execution buttons to mimic dressed ashlar masonry blocks.

## Components

### Buttons & Action Pedestals
- **Primary Sovereign Action:** Basalt black fill (`#121110`), crisp white-limestone text (`#FBF9F5`), `0px` radius, hairline internal border (`#9E7D47`). Padding: `14px 28px`. In hover state, the background shifts to `#1C1B19` with the border expanding to an outer hairline rule offset by `2px`.
- **Secondary Chiseled Action:** Transparent background with a `1px` solid basalt border (`#1C1B19`), basalt text, and `label-technical` tracking. On hover: fills with pale limestone (`#F4EFE6`).
- **Bronze Seal Action:** Imperial bronze outline (`#9E7D47`) with antique gold text (`#B89758`). Used exclusively for decisive institutional transitions, authorizations, and protocol triggers.

### Architectural Inscription Badges & Chips
- Designed as chiseled stone tags rather than casual web pills.
- Strictly rectangular, bounded by `1px` borders in bronze (`#9E7D47`) or weathered ash (`#54514B`).
- Typography is strictly uppercase `label-inscription` or `label-technical`, preceded by a Roman numeral or classical glyph (`§`, `I`, `•`).
- Background: `#F4EFE6` (limestone) or subtle basalt wash.

### Form Fields & Inputs
- **Base Input:** Travertine background with a hairline bottom border of weathered ash (`#54514B`) or full four-side hairline border of honed mortar (`#E8DFD1`). No rounded corners.
- **Focus State:** Bottom hairline transforms into a `2px` stroke of imperial bronze (`#9E7D47`). No soft glow or focus ring; the transition is abrupt and crisp.
- **Labels:** Positioned atop the input frame in uppercase `label-technical`, accompanied by an index code (e.g., `REF. 001 // SYSTEM PARAMETER`).

### Structural Cards & Bays
- Reject elevated SaaS card patterns. Instead, construct "Architectural Bays": continuous rectangular fields delimited by `1px` hairlines with zero drop shadow.
- Header bands within bays are demarcated by a horizontal hairline architrave and carry a left-aligned Roman numeral followed by a serif section title.
- Bay footers feature metadata stamps (timestamps, cryptographic signatures, or authority seals) in `label-technical`.

### Data Tables & Colonnade Lists
- Headers set in uppercase `label-technical` with an imperial bronze top and bottom rule.
- Rows separated by hairline mortar rules (`#E8DFD1`). Alternating row backgrounds are prohibited; distinction relies solely on line discipline and micro-spacing.
- Numerical data sets must use tabular figures with monospace alignment for uncompromising auditability.

### Checkboxes & Selectors
- Checkboxes are strict square frames (`16px × 16px`) with `1px` basalt borders. When active, they display a solid basalt inner square (`8px × 8px`) or a sharp bronze cross (`×`), rejecting rounded checkmarks.
- Radio buttons employ a square-within-a-square architectural target, reinforcing the non-curvilinear geometry of the system.
