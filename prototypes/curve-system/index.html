Chinvat Bridge / motion lines / prototype

One geometry for every line that moves.

Every bend in the wire, the diagram connectors and the currents becomes one kind of turn: curvature rises from zero, peaks at the role's radius and falls back to zero. Turns are joined by straight runs at 0, 45 and 90 degrees, on the layout grid. Below, the system is set against the current geometry, using the homepage's real layout measured at four widths.

Isolated prototype. Nothing in production imports it.

Principles

The wire should read as engineered, like a survey line or a railway alignment, not freehand. A small set of rules is enough to get there.

Straight runs, measured turns

A line is straight runs joined by turns. It never curves over its whole length. Curvature belongs to the turns, so the straights read as structure and the turns as decisions.

Transition curves only

Every turn eases in and out: curvature grows linearly from zero to 1/R and back (an Euler spiral, as on railways and motorways). No turn starts abruptly, so the moving head never jolts where a straight meets a bend.

One radius per role

The wire turns at one radius, set from the page margin so it scales with the layout. Diagram connectors turn at one radius in board units, so they scale with the artwork. Nothing else turns.

Three headings

Runs go down, across, or down at 45 degrees. A small offset takes a shallower S at the same radius. Diagonals stay parallel wherever they occur, echoing the bridge deck in the Method plates.

Lines live on the grid

Vertical runs take the page margin's centre, the column gutters or a port's own line. Lane changes sit in the middle of the gap they cross, not hard against the text.

Arrive straight

A line leaves and reaches a port on a straight lead of at least half its radius. It never lands mid-curve and never hooks into a port.

Curve families

Four patterns cover every line that carries the current: the quarter turn, the lane change (45° diagonals and shallower S-curves), the port landing and the through-route. Each card draws the current construction beside the proposed one with the same ends, then plots curvature along the length. A step in the plot is a visible break. A spike is a kink.

Inside the diagrams

The Automation plate's connectors use rounded elbows drawn as quadratic curves. A quadratic is a parabola, not an arc, so its "6 unit" corner swings from a 12 unit radius at the ends to 4.2 at the apex, with a curvature step at both joins. The cobalt current and the saffron run travel these lines, so the break shows in motion.

What stays sharp

  • Annotation: the hero's dotted brackets, the Method gauges and the ruler follow drafting convention. Dimension and bracket lines meet at true corners. Rounding them would blur flow lines into notes.
  • Data: the SEO trace keeps its vertices, because each one is a reading. The ambient run along it is a data cursor, not a flow.
  • Projected stone: the deck line, inlay and slab edges are projections of the 3D bridge. Their geometry belongs to the scene.
  • Graph links: the AI plate's spokes and rim are straight node-to-node links.

The rule: a line that carries the current (wire, connector, pulse route) uses the curve system. A line that measures, plots or depicts does not.

Motion, kept apart from geometry

Geometry produces a path and its length table. Motion reads only lengths and tokens. Today the ambient runs use pathLength="100" with one duration, so the speed and comet size follow the line's length. The plate pulses all take 0.68 s whatever their length. Proposed: one speed per role, so the duration comes from the length.

Current: one duration

Three runs of different lengths, each lasting 2.4 s, with a dash of 10% of the line. The longest moves 3.5 times faster than the shortest, with a comet 3.5 times longer.

Proposed: one speed

The same lines at the ambient flow speed, with the wire's comet (16 px head, 44 and 84 px tail). Short lines finish sooner, and every comet looks the same.

Tokens

Geometry and motion tokens are kept separate. Radii derive from tokens the site already has, so the curve system follows any change to the page margin or the artwork's scale.
